# Lamps (the game's point and spot lights)

The data side, meaning what a LIGHT record holds and where the lights are placed, is in
[formats/lights.md](formats/lights.md). This page covers how the renderer draws them. The claims say where they come from: *from the code*,
*measured* (how), or *open*.

The game draws these lights in both looks, so this is not a Faithful / Meitou switch. The bench's `lights` A/B switch turns them off (the former `--lights off` option, which the parity gates used, was removed).

## What is drawn

*From the code* (`WorldLamps`, `LampShaders`):

- **Which lights.** The lights of every placed building and its exterior/interior layouts (`WorldLights.ForWorld`), read on a worker at start, plus the lights of buildings placed during play while they are complete (`WorldLamps.SetRuntime`, from the host's building diff, [simulation](simulation.md#buildings); placed as the world's, no interior layouts). The game switches a building's lights on when it completes ([construction tick](game/buildings-production.md#construction-tick)).
  Interior lamps enter the grid only with a loaded owner mask (matched by the building prefix of their placement id). Their direct light is
  clipped to its convex footprint and height range with a0.5-unit surface tolerance, no clipping while the lamps are traced (the rays stop the light at the walls; **Observed** bar set: the hull trimmed the wall round a door into a dark polygon) (see [zones](formats/zones.md#interior-masks)). Untraced, a surface lit as an interior (the room's walls and furniture, `giInterior`) takes its room's lamps 40 units past the footprint (`WorldLamps.RoomInteriorSlack`, `LAMP_ROOM_SLACK`); the rest keep the tight tolerance, so the street stays dark (**Observed**, save lamp, the Storm House's teal tubes, lamp shadows off: the dark polygon round the doorway gone, the street through the door unchanged).
  Masks above32 edges are unsupported; direct lamps have no occluder shadows within that hull.
  - Night-only lamps (`WorldLightRule.Night`: every building not indoors, [lights](formats/lights.md#when-a-light-is-on)) are left out while the daylight factor is above 0.1;
    the rest are always on. The `interiorlamps` switch (Meitou, default) keeps interior-layout lamps on by day too; Faithful: night only.
    Player lamps placed during play are not interior lamps. A placed building's lamps are scaled by its power share (`SetRuntimePower`) and left out at 0.
  - Lanterns characters carry (`CharacterAppearance.Lights`, [lights](formats/lights.md#carried-lanterns)) are posed with the character each frame
    (`CharacterRenderer.CollectLamps`) and binned after the others as `WorldLamps.Moving`; they are night-only.
- **Binning.** Each frame, the lights whose reach overlaps a 64 × 64 grid of 128-unit cells around the eye (8192 units across, at most
  1024 lights) are written to three textures, one set per frame slot:
  - `uLightCells` (RG32F): each cell's first entry and count;
  - `uLightIndex` (R32F): the light numbers, 1024 per row;
  - `uLightData` (RGBA32F): a row of37 texels per light: position/radius; radiance/type; spot direction/outer cosine;
    inner cosine/falloff/room edge count/emitter id; room min/max height; up to32 inward footprint planes (normal X/Z, offset, 0).
    Edge count0 means an exterior lamp; unused room texels are ignored.

  `uLightGrid` holds the grid's corner, its cell size and its cells per side. Its w is 0 when there is nothing to draw, and then the
  shaders skip the lamps entirely.
- **Shading.** `kenshiLight` adds every light of the pixel's cell, lit the way the sun is: diffuse `π · N·L · radiance · 0.96` times the
  albedo, plus the same GGX specular as the sun's. Here `radiance = colour · power · Attenuation(d, radius) [· SpotFactor]`, with the
  game's attenuation and spot cone (`WorldLights.Attenuation` / `SpotFactor`, ported to GLSL). There are no shadows, as in the game, unless the [`lampshadows`](#traced-lamp-shadows) switch is on.
  - **Lamp warmth** (`lampwarmth` switch, Meitou, default, on the Advanced page; `LampWarmth`). Faithful: the LIGHT `diffuse` as is. Meitou: a lamp whose blue is its
    weakest channel and whose green is above 0.808 of its red (the torches' FFCE5E ratio) has its green lowered halfway to that ratio, and its blue by
    the same factor, weighted by `(min(r, g) − b) / max` over 0.25 (capped at 1) times 0.5, then scaled back to its Rec. 709 luminance. Hues:
    `indoor_lamp_light` E9FF9B → FFF393, `spotlight powerful` F1FFC4 → FFF7BE, `street_light_yellow` F1E8B4 → FFE4B1; torches, fires and the
    cyan lamps are unchanged. Applied where the rows are written, so the probes, tiles and the editor bar's fill follow.
  - Pulsing and shimmering lights get the game's effect factor on the CPU, from a clock in seconds × 60.
- **Native and legacy.** The legacy programs take the uniform and the three samplers by name. The native frame block has `lightGrid` at
  8608 and the three bindless indices at 8624, 8628 and 8632.
- **Probe GI** ([render-gi.md](render-gi.md)). The probe trace binds the same textures (bindings 10–12, `uLightGrid` in its parameters)
  and adds the lights' diffuse light at each hit (`LAMP_DIFFUSE_ONLY`), so lamp light bounces too. Interior lamps additionally use
  a ray from the hit towards the lamp; intervening geometry blocks the bounce source. The lamp's owning fixture is excluded from its
  own shadow query (light points may be embedded in it); all other geometry still occludes. Direct raster lamps retain the game's unshadowed
  shading within the building mask. Interiors keep probe lighting; there is no ambient override.

## Traced lamp shadows
- Hair (`CharacterShaders`: bit 21 on what is no character receiver, no metal) is a receiver of the lamps' rays alone: its ray starts 0.15 units towards the lamp (`lampLift`) and passes every hair card (`lampBlocker`: a character's record whose tint has no alpha; the ray takes every geometry as a candidate), so a hat, a hood, the head and the room shadow it and its own cards do not. **Verified** (save lamp, the studio's hooded man with beard 44, `beard.sh`, before and after): started 1.5 units up the ray, past its cards, it also started past the hood's edge, and the beard was lit in the hood's shadow (the owner's report: a beard glowing in a face a hat kept dark); now the part under the hood is dark and the part hanging in the lamp's light is lit.
- Before a round's rays (`LampShadowPass.TraceRound`) the flags and the filter's scratch are cleared to "no receiver": the filter reads round a pixel and took another lamp's flags in from cells outside the lamp's own (**Observed**, owner: darker 64-pixel squares beside a shadow). **Observed**: 0.55 ms for 37 lamps when each lamp cleared two full-size targets.
- The penumbra filter reads each pixel's plane from a target made once a frame (`PenumbraFilter.EnsurePlanes`) instead of rebuilding a tap's plane from five depths at every tap. **Observed** (heavy-scene, 2560x1440, 24 lamp areas): the lamps' pass 8.0 to about 6.5 ms.

The `lampshadows` switch, off by default and in no look (`PostOptions.LampShadows`, `PostProcess.LampShadows.cs`, `Gi/LampShadowPass.cs`, `Gi/LampShadowShaders.cs`).
The game has none: this is Meitou's. From KenshiNative's traced lamp shadows (near-skip), adapted from a term in the light shader to a pass over the forward scene.

- **Pass.** After the GI resolve, before the shafts and fog volumes: no world shader changes, nothing runs or is allocated while the switch is off. A compute pass writes, per pixel of the render size, per traced lamp (in lamp index order) whether it is blocked and its light per unit of albedo; the filter softens each lamp's blocked flag on its own, reading only neighbours that trace the same pair of lamps (**Observed** in saves lamp-leak and spotlight-cross: averaging the light left an orange line along a wall's foot and on the ripples, and one share of two lamps together left a pale mottled trail moving with a character). The filter (shared with the traced sun) also leaves out taps on a surface turned away, and a pixel on such a crease keeps its own ray. A full-screen pass takes that, times the stored albedo and the haze's transmittance, off the scene colour (reverse-subtract blend), then a max blend with 0 keeps the scene above black, since the albedo is stored rounded. No spatial filter and no history of its own.
- **Inert** without ray queries or storage for RGBA16F, without the surface target (`GiResolve` above 0, so the `gi` switch and probes on, not `--no-gi`), by day when no lamp is in the grid, and on Metal (`gpu.Post` is null there).
- **Which lamps.** The pass includes `lampLight` itself and defines `LAMP_VISIBILITY` to note each lamp's `radiance * diffuseNl`, so the selection has the world's attenuation, spot cone and room clip by construction. The strongest 2 by their light on the drawn plane (attenuation, cone and the plane from the depth: a normal's bumps must not change a pixel's lamps; KenshiNative's choice of 2 on cost) are traced; weaker lamps stay unshadowed.
- **The ray.** One per lamp, from the surface raised along its drawn plane (from the depth, not the normal map: per-pixel bumps moved the origins and frayed the edges, **Observed** in play) by at least 0.5 units or two pixels' width, to the lamp's centre, the same every frame: no noise for the upscaler to average and nothing to trail (KenshiNative sampled a 1-unit disc over 2 x 2 blocks moving each frame). A pixel no lamp lights (turned away from them, out of reach) is no receiver of the filter, as the sun's. The lamp's size is the [penumbra filter](render-shadows.md#traced-sun)'s: radius 2 units (a flame or a bulb in its glass), so the blocked lamps' widest penumbra at the receiver is 2 x the blocker's distance over its distance to the lamp. Rays go through the frame's TLAS and pass cut-out texels (`TracedSunShaders.CasterCutout`). Lamp data is this frame's, so carried lanterns are traced where they are drawn.
- **A lamp's own fixture.** A ray stops short of the lamp and at its radius: 15 units short (KenshiNative's near-skip) for a lamp without an emitter id, 2 source radii (2 to 5 units, `LampTiles.SkipOf`, `lampSkipOf`; the tiles' rays leave out the same) for one with an id, whose housing is passed by name. With 15 units for all, what stood within 1.5 m of an interior lamp cast nothing from its near part: a barrel by a lamp post left the floor lit under it up to where its far part began to cast (**Observed**, the Hub's thieves' tower: a lit crescent between the barrel's foot and its shadow before, the shadow at the foot after). Interior lamps also pass through their emitter id's geometry (`GeometryRecord.Emitter`), as the probe trace does. Exterior lamps have no id: their placement is often the whole building, whose walls should block them.
- **Specular stays, except on characters.** On the world's surfaces only diffuse is removed: the surface target has no gloss, so the GGX term cannot be recomputed there, and a lamp's highlight survives in its shadow (a dielectric's is small). A character's fragment writes the levels of the lamps' diffuse and specular it took into the sun term's alpha ([packing](render-shadows.md#traced-sun)); its pixel notes its lamps' light even when nothing blocks it. The apply takes the diffuse's lost share (lost over all lamps' light), rebuilt from the stored albedo and the shading normal and scaled to the fragment's own level (the channels' mean: matched by luminance, a rebuilt hue short of green took three times as much red). The specular is rebuilt lamp by lamp in the light kernel (stored normal and gloss; `uPlain`: rgb what is lost, a the luminance of all) and the apply takes lost / all of the fragment's own: each lamp's hue and share. Taken in the diffuse's hue, skin's shine round the eyes, nose and lips went cyan; taken by the diffuse's share in the lost light's hue (skin's red scattering band), or read back below what the fragment took, a highlight left green pixels on a face in a lamp's shadow (**Observed**, save `green`: 115-190 green pixels a frame before, 0-67 after, the rest mostly dim ambient; a few bright ones still show on some frames, cause **Unknown**). Without the removal a blocked lamp's highlight and a red glow stayed on a face in a hood.
- **Casters.** Rays see everything the TLAS holds (`TracedSunShaders.LampMask`): terrain, objects (their cut-out parts by their own maps), foliage of radius 150 or more within 4000 units of the eye, and, built while the pass runs (lamps in the grid), the small foliage (radius 6 and up within 3000) and the grass (within 1200, or the [traced sun](render-shadows.md#traced-sun)'s reach) as for the traced sun, and the characters. Grass, small foliage and characters are absent from the probes' rays (`GiMask`).
- **Lamps among grass** (`LampShadowPass.PlanLampArea`, `TracedGrass.Map`, `grassMayShade` in `LampShadowShaders.Classify`): with the grass traced, a
  lamp within 500 units of the eye (plus its radius) with a traced page of blades in its reach (`TracedGrass.Within`, by the pages' squares) is a
  grass lamp. Its area carries two cell masks: the movers' shadow boxes (`Cover`, as for any lamp) and its whole reach; the classification asks
  the movers' test only in the first and the grass's only in the second (asked over the whole reach the movers' cone test kept 2.5 times the
  cells: the cost was those cells, not the rays crossing grass). The grass's test is exact against a map of the traced blades: per 16-unit cell
  of a 512-cell window round the eye, the highest top of a traced card over it (the cards as expanded into their structures, so a blade cut by
  an interior mask is not there; a swaying patch's widened by twice its sway), and nine coarser levels of maxima; made again only when the traced
  patches or their structures change (at most 0.08 ms when made every frame). A cell is asked against the hull of its frustum (the pyramid's depths)
  and the lamp, which holds every segment from a receiver to the lamp (taken a few units and two pixels lower and wider, where the rays leave):
  a point of the hull is a share k of the way to the lamp, so across the ground it is in the frustum's box moved that share and its height is at
  least the frustum's lowest plus that share of the rise to the lamp; a map cell is kept when a share brings the moved box over it with a blade
  top above that height, and a coarse cell that is kept is asked again in its four finer ones, down to 16 units. **Observed** (RTX 4090,
  2560x1440, paused): a lit hall standing on grass pages, lamp stage 9.8 -> 8.2 ms (7.2 with the grass not traced, 7.5 with the grass never asked);
  city-night 1.80 -> 1.71; a desert town at night 3.82 -> 3.66 by the pages' test alone. **Verified** (2026-11-17, same session A/B, 6 s settled):
  city-night and "The boys 123" at night, the pixels the traced grass changes are the same with and without the map (mean 0.42 against a 0.40
  floor); the hall at full size the same (157 pixels over 8/255 against 205 between two frames); at half size the map is nearer the full-size
  picture (mean 0.277 against 0.321): without it the own-ray list overflowed. Blocks of 16 or 32 pixels with their own depths instead of the
  64-pixel cell: no gain beyond the noise, and +0.24 ms in city-night (16 tests a cell).
- **Characters** (`TracedCharacters`, `CharacterRenderer.Rays`, mask `0x40`). Built for the lamps and the [traced sun](render-shadows.md#traced-sun). The nearest 128 within 4000 of the eye whose shadow can be in view, at most 3M triangles, at the level the colour pass draws (a surface's ray leaves the triangles it drew), with every shown part (clothes, armour, carried and drawn weapons, animals' meshes). A kernel skins each mesh group with the update's palettes, as the vertex program does (face morphs, bones, the look's hidden limbs and parts, headless, the boots' side), a triangle's three corners apiece; a triangle with a hidden corner collapses to a point. Per character one bottom level (fast build, refittable), refitted each frame its pose is new and built again every 60 to 75 refits or when its parts or levels change; its instance sits at the character's position. A character whose pose is held (frozen, or thinned by its size on screen: `CharState.Posed` unchanged) and whose pieces lie as they were skinned (within 0.05 units, same hidden parts and morphs) is neither skinned nor refitted. **Observed** (RTX 4090, 2560 x 1440, a crowd of 116 characters, 835k triangles): 50 held, 1.07 -> 0.80 ms of GPU; of that the kernel is 0.19 and the refits 0.61, which follow the triangles refitted (one command for all of them gained nothing). Hair is cut by its map's channel and threshold, items clipped on their normal map's alpha at 0.6, as the shadow draws. The top level is refitted while characters are in it, built again every 30 frames or when the instances change. The kernel runs one invocation a triangle over all of them (a job found by its first triangle). **Observed** (RTX 4090, 1920 x 1080 with DLSS, the new-game start in the Hub with `--crowd 60`, 110 characters, 326k triangles at drawn levels): 0.5 ms GPU a frame skinning and refitting, 0.6 with the top level, 62 MB; a lone close-up character (38k triangles) 0.4 ms. The GPU was shared with another job: runs varied 2-4x.
- **Areas.** With the tiles the rays see the movers alone, and each lamp's pass works on the 64-pixel cells of the screen a mover's shadow can fall on (`LampShadowPass.PlanAreas`, `Classify`), from the mover's sphere. A character's sphere is its body's grown round every shown part that hangs from a bone, a tenth more (`CharacterRenderer.RayCharacterOf`): with the body's radius alone the shadow of a carried pole or a sheathed sword fell outside its cells and was cut along them, in blocks that came and went as characters walked. **Observed** (the Hub's first building at night, 2560 x 1440, 12 paused moments against spheres four times as large): the body's radius left 1774 and 665 pixels wrong in two of them; the grown sphere at most 232, the level of two captures of one sphere.
- **Characters as receivers.** While the pass will run (`GiProbes.LampReceivers`, `uGiParams.z` bit 16) a character's fragment writes its normal and albedo to the surface target with bit 19 (`kenshiLight`, `atmoReceiver` 3), which this pass alone reads (`LAMP_RECEIVER`): a hood shades the face, an arm the flank. Its rays leave as the [traced sun](render-shadows.md#traced-sun)'s do on a body (`lampBody`): close to the skin and past the creases between facets (`BodyContact`), and from under a facet turned from the lamp the ray crosses the body and is asked for blockers from where it leaves (`BodyChord`). Hair is no receiver (its cards shadowed each other black); it casts on the body as on everything else (a fringe shades the forehead). **Observed** (the editor's bar, a bare Greenlander and a clothed Scorchlander, 2560 x 1440): chin, hair mass and arms shade the body with no lines or facets; Skin's whole lamp term is taken back, its band past the terminator too: the fragment writes its skin share and its row of the scattering table (bits 28-31 and 20-27) and the pass computes the colour pass's own term (`LampShadowShaders.SkinTerm`, the shading normal standing for the smoothed ones). With N.L alone the band stayed and skin in a lamp's shadow glowed red (**Observed**, the editor's bar, a hooded face: red over green of a shadowed cheek 1.60 before, 1.44 after; 1.53 to 1.44 on a bare cheek). With the tiles every lamp a mover stands in is traced, one at a time (`LampShadowPass.RunPerLamp`); without them a pixel traces its two strongest and the others keep both. A worn receiver (surface bit 20, no skin share) turned from the lamp is in its own shadow (`lampWorn` in `lampBlocker`) instead of crossing to the far side as a body does: crossed, a hood's two layers let the lamp through onto its inside's weave. `lampSkinDiffuse` uses one normal for all three channels, as the pass cannot rebuild the sun term's per-channel normals: with them a blocked lamp left red and green patches along the normal map's bumps, a face that looked painted (**Observed**). **Observed** (editor's bar at night, a hooded character, against a path-traced reference of the same scene, local tooling not in the repo; this scene only): inside of the hood's inner fold 0.024 -> 0.008 luminance (reference 0.015 with its cloth sheen, 0.006 without). Not checked: dark facets near the terminator on a lit cloth. Cost **Unknown**.
- **A facet turned from the lamp** on a body (its smooth shading still lit): the ray starts under it and looks for the body's way out within 0.8 units (`BodyChord`), then for blockers from there. With no way out that near the body itself is in the way and the lamp is blocked (`lampBlocker`). Left to the blocker rays, which skip the backs of faces, such a ray left the body by its far side unseen: the ribs' facets under a breast stayed lit inside the breast's shadow, as lit and dark triangles along its edge (**Observed**, the editor's bar, a naked woman with Breast size 160, three views: angular wedges before, one shadow with a round edge after; a hooded, clothed one unchanged).
- **Not covered.** Foliage, grass, hair, impostors and water write 0 to the surface target, so they keep unshadowed lamp light (interior surfaces too with `interiorgi` Faithful). Construction sites, impostor-only objects, objects past 6000 and foliage past 4000 (small 3000) of the eye, debris (bolts, dropped items), birds and particles cast nothing. The TLAS's meshes and 16-unit terrain grid are coarser than what is drawn: **Unknown** whether grazing rays show acne or leaks.

## Lamp tiles

The still world's lamp shadows (`Gi/LampTiles`, console `lamps tiles on|off`): every lamp of a pixel is shadowed, with no limit on their number. Characters are not in the tiles.

- **Tiles.** Each lamp near the eye holds a distance tile traced from the lamp, in one atlas (8192², R16), and carried lamps a tile traced whole each frame (16 at most, first come).
- **Mapping.** A cone on its plane, a point lamp or a cone over 150° by octahedral directions. Side from reach and cone for about 1.25 units per texel at the end of the reach, halved past 1500 and 3500 units from the eye.
- **Invalidation.** `GiScene` keeps a hash per 128-unit cell of the object and foliage instances, so anything placed, removed or moved (a zone streamed in, a building built or ruined, a door) retraces the lamps whose reach holds that cell, 3 M rays a frame at most. An object that has moved in the last 60 frames is out of the tiles (instance mask `Moving`, what the probes and the sun still see) and in the per-pixel rays, so what keeps moving retraces nothing and a door's shadow follows it. A tile that must be retraced waits 2 frames between traces within 600 units of the eye, 16 beyond.
- **Ground.** Rays leave the ground where they come out of it (front faces culled): the traced grid is coarser than the drawn ground, and the first hit along a graze shaded the ground behind it (**Observed** in save spotlight-cross, as dark arcs round each lamp). Against 256 rays to the lamp's disc (console `lamps refview`), **Observed**: a lamp's shadow of objects matches (0.09 of 255 mean difference for a lamp post); a shadow of the ground's relief from a searchlight is narrower than the truth (6 of 255), and taking where the ray went into the ground for the penumbra's width made it worse (9.6).
- **Cost of the lookup.** Every lit fragment of the world runs the 48-lookup search per lamp, then 16 to 256 lookups of filter where it found a blocker. **Observed** (2560 x 1440 native, `lamps tiles off`): 1.5 ms of a town at night (objects 2.34 -> 1.27 ms, terrain 1.59 -> 1.19), 1.35 ms of objects in a lit interior. When all 48 lookups of the search are blocked and the penumbra is no wider than the search, the lamp is taken as hidden and the filter skipped: objects 2.46 -> 2.20, terrain 1.59 -> 1.52 in that town, the picture at the noise floor. The loops' counts are constants on purpose: a count that follows the search radius (16 to 48) stopped the compiler folding the spiral's cosines and sines and cost every lit fragment 0.75 ms more (**Observed**).
- **Penumbra.** The source radius comes from the reach (0.005 of it, 1 to 2.5 units; the LIGHT record has no size field, **Verified** in `fcs.def` and [lights](formats/lights.md)). A spiral finds the blockers, a second one filters; a lookup r texels off counts only what stands nearer the lamp by what the surface can gain over r texels (its plane's depth at each lookup swung with the normal at a graze, and drew the ripples and the terrain's facets into the penumbra, **Observed**). Coarse levels of nearest blockers were tried and dropped: they read a slope's own near edge as a blocker, and drew squares (**Observed**, spotlight-cross).
- **Characters and moving objects.** Each lamp apart (`LampShadowPass.RunPerLamp`): over the part of the screen the movers' shadows from that lamp can reach, per-pixel rays, the penumbra filter on that one lamp's flags, and that lamp's light after the still world added to one sum taken off the scene. The rays' kernel asks only whether the lamp reaches the pixel (reach, cone, facing); the lamp's light after the still world (its tile's penumbra search and filter, the costly part) is computed by the last kernel, only where the filtered flag is above 0. A pixel the still world already shadows now carries its mover's flag into the filter, where it used to carry 0. The kernels run over lists of 64-pixel cells, not the area's rectangle (`LampShadowShaders.Classify`, an indirect dispatch a kernel): a cell is listed when a mover's shadow box covers it and what this frame's depth pyramid shows in it (built after the opaque scene when the lamps' shadows run, `PostProcess.BuildHiz`) can lie within the lamp's reach and inside the shadow cone of a mover in that reach (a sphere round the cell's frustum between its nearest and farthest depth; over 64 such movers, or without a pyramid, the boxes alone decide), and so are its eight neighbours (the filter reads up to 30 pixels round a pixel). **Observed** (2560x1440, town at night, 19 lamps' areas, 54 movers): the stage 2.9 -> 2.45 ms with the light computed last, -> 1.78 ms with the lists (0.32 ms with no cell listed, 2.56 with every cell); spotlight-cross 2.5 -> 1.86 ms. A mover standing at its lamp (a carried lantern) keeps every cell in the lamp's reach.
- **Layers and rounds** (`PlanLayers`, `TraceRound`, `LampShadowShaders.Classify`). A lamp at a time cost four small dispatches a lamp, each waited for: **Observed** 3.1 ms for a town's 52 lamps whose cells cover 4.8 screens, 3.9 ms for 69 lamps over 2.7 screens (57 µs a lamp). Lamps whose cells neither overlap nor touch share a layer; a round works on up to `MaxSlices` 8 layers at once (1 at the full size), each in its slice of the rays' targets (RG16F arrays: blocked, penumbra radius or -1). `Classify` writes per round a list of rays (cell, slice, lamp row), a map (slice, cell -> lamp row + 1) and a list of cells without repeats. A round is one dispatch each for the rays, the filter's rows and columns, and the light, which is a gather: a thread a pixel of the round's cells loops the slices' lamps there and adds their lost light once.
- **Half size** (`LampShadowPass.HalfMode`, 2). The rays and the filter run on one pixel of each 2 x 2 (mode 2: each of the four in turn over four frames while a temporal resolve runs; 1: always the same; 0: every pixel, one slice a round). The light kernel, at the render size, takes a pixel's flag from the 1, 2 or 4 half-size flags it lies between that are of its kind (world, skin, hair, worn) and within 1.5 pixels (5 mm at least) of its plane; a 4 x 4 search when none is. A pixel of another kind than its 2 x 2's traced pixel, or off its plane, is listed by the rays' kernel and traced by `TraceOwn` (a thread a pixel; R32UI `uExact`): without it shadowed skin seen between a moustache's lit strands took the strands' flags (**Observed**, the studio's hooded man with beard 44). Traced inside the rays' kernel those pixels kept their blocks' other threads waiting (+1.5 ms in a crowd, +1.1 as a pass of their own). The light kernel holds no ray query: one that never ran still cost 1.4 ms of 6. The half-size filter's radius is sqrt(R^2 - 0.5) half pixels, 0.5 at least (no blur): the half size spreads an edge by itself, and with the full-size radius contact shadows were twice as soft (**Observed**).
- **Observed** (2560 x 1440 native, paused, per-lamp full size against rounds at half size, same session): the lamps' pass 9.2 -> 5.9 ms in the save heavy-scene (37 lamps, 126 M pixel-lamps, 5 rounds), 3.07 -> 1.3 ms in city-night (52 lamps, 4 rounds), 3.9 -> 1.2 ms at Blister Hill by night (69 lamps).

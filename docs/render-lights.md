# Lamps (the game's point and spot lights)

The data side, meaning what a LIGHT record holds and where the lights are placed, is in
[formats/lights.md](formats/lights.md). This page covers how the renderer draws them. The claims say where they come from: *from the code*,
*measured* (how), or *open*.

The game draws these lights in both looks, so this is not a Faithful / Meitou switch. The bench's `lights` A/B switch turns them off (the former `--lights off` option, which the parity gates used, was removed).

## What is drawn

*From the code* (`WorldLamps`, `LampShaders`):

- **Which lights.** The lights of every placed building and its exterior/interior layouts (`WorldLights.ForWorld`), read on a worker at start, plus the lights of buildings placed during play while they are complete (`WorldLamps.SetRuntime`, from the host's building diff, [simulation](simulation.md#buildings); placed as the world's, no interior layouts). The game switches a building's lights on when it completes ([construction tick](game/buildings-production.md#construction-tick)).
  Interior lamps enter the grid only with a loaded owner mask (matched by the building prefix of their placement id). Their direct light is
  clipped to its convex footprint and height range with a0.5-unit surface tolerance, no clipping while the lamps are traced (the rays stop the light at the walls; **Observed** bar set: the hull trimmed the wall round a door into a dark polygon) (see [zones](formats/zones.md#interior-masks)).
  Masks above32 edges are unsupported; direct lamps have no occluder shadows within that hull.
  - Night-only lamps (`WorldLightRule.Night`: every building not indoors, [lights](formats/lights.md#when-a-light-is-on)) are left out while the daylight factor is above 0.1;
    the rest are always on. The `interiorlamps` switch (Meitou, default) keeps interior-layout lamps on by day too; Faithful: night only.
    Player lamps placed during play are not interior lamps. A placed building's lamps are scaled by its power share (`SetRuntimePower`) and left out at 0. World lamps
    take no power factor: the town buildings' power and on state are **Unknown** ([lights](formats/lights.md#when-a-light-is-on)).
  - Lanterns characters carry (`CharacterAppearance.Lights`, [lights](formats/lights.md#carried-lanterns)) are posed with the character each frame
    (`CharacterRenderer.CollectLamps`) and binned after the others as `WorldLamps.Moving`; they are night-only.
- **Binning.** Each frame, the lights whose reach overlaps a 64 × 64 grid of 128-unit cells around the eye (8192 units across, at most
  1024 lights) are written to three textures, one set per frame slot:
  - `uLightCells` (RG32F): each cell's first entry and count;
  - `uLightIndex` (R32F): the light numbers, 1024 per row;
  - `uLightData` (RGBA32F): a row of37 texels per light: position/radius; radiance/type; spot direction/outer cosine;
    inner cosine/falloff/room edge count/emitter id; room min/max height; up to32 inward footprint planes (normal X/Z, offset, 0).
    Edge count0 means an exterior lamp; unused room texels are ignored.

  `uLightGrid` holds the grid's corner, its cell size and its cells per side. Its w is 0 when there is nothing to draw, and then the
  shaders skip the lamps entirely.
- **Shading.** `kenshiLight` adds every light of the pixel's cell, lit the way the sun is: diffuse `π · N·L · radiance · 0.96` times the
  albedo, plus the same GGX specular as the sun's. Here `radiance = colour · power · Attenuation(d, radius) [· SpotFactor]`, with the
  game's attenuation and spot cone (`WorldLights.Attenuation` / `SpotFactor`, ported to GLSL). There are no shadows, as in the game, unless the [`lampshadows`](#traced-lamp-shadows) switch is on.
  - Pulsing and shimmering lights get the game's effect factor on the CPU, from a clock in seconds × 60. The unit of the game's clock
    value is **Unknown**.
- **Native and legacy.** The legacy programs take the uniform and the three samplers by name. The native frame block has `lightGrid` at
  8608 and the three bindless indices at 8624, 8628 and 8632 (`FrameConstants` is 8640 bytes).
- **Probe GI** ([render-gi.md](render-gi.md)). The probe trace binds the same textures (bindings 10–12, `uLightGrid` in its parameters)
  and adds the lights' diffuse light at each hit (`LAMP_DIFFUSE_ONLY`), so lamp light bounces too. Interior lamps additionally use
  a ray from the hit towards the lamp; intervening geometry blocks the bounce source. The lamp's owning fixture is excluded from its
  own shadow query (light points may be embedded in it); all other geometry still occludes. Direct raster lamps retain the game's unshadowed
  shading within the building mask. Interiors keep probe lighting; there is no ambient override.

## Traced lamp shadows

The `lampshadows` switch, off by default and in no look (`PostOptions.LampShadows`, `PostProcess.LampShadows.cs`, `Gi/LampShadowPass.cs`, `Gi/LampShadowShaders.cs`).
The game has none: this is Meitou's. From KenshiNative's traced lamp shadows (near-skip), adapted from a term in the light shader to a pass over the forward scene.
*From the code*, none of it run on a GPU: look and cost are **Unknown**.

- **Pass.** After the GI resolve, before the shafts and fog volumes: no world shader changes, nothing runs or is allocated while the switch is off. A compute pass writes, per pixel of the render size, per traced lamp (in lamp index order) whether it is blocked and its light per unit of albedo; the filter softens each lamp's blocked flag on its own, reading only neighbours that trace the same pair of lamps (**Observed** in saves lamp-leak and spotlight-cross: averaging the light left an orange line along a wall's foot and on the ripples, and one share of two lamps together left a pale mottled trail moving with a character). The filter (shared with the traced sun) also leaves out taps on a surface turned away, and a pixel on such a crease keeps its own ray. A full-screen pass takes that, times the stored albedo and the haze's transmittance, off the scene colour (reverse-subtract blend), then a max blend with 0 keeps the scene above black, since the albedo is stored rounded. No spatial filter and no history of its own.
- **Inert** without ray queries or storage for RGBA16F, without the surface target (`GiResolve` above 0, so the `gi` switch and probes on, not `--no-gi`), by day when no lamp is in the grid, and on Metal (`gpu.Post` is null there).
- **Which lamps.** The pass includes `lampLight` itself and defines `LAMP_VISIBILITY` to note each lamp's `radiance * diffuseNl`, so the selection has the world's attenuation, spot cone and room clip by construction. The strongest 2 by their light on the drawn plane (attenuation, cone and the plane from the depth: a normal's bumps must not change a pixel's lamps; KenshiNative's choice of 2 on cost) are traced; weaker lamps stay unshadowed.
- **The ray.** One per lamp, from the surface raised along its drawn plane (from the depth, not the normal map: per-pixel bumps moved the origins and frayed the edges, **Observed** in play) by at least 0.5 units or two pixels' width, to the lamp's centre, the same every frame: no noise for the upscaler to average and nothing to trail (KenshiNative sampled a 1-unit disc over 2 x 2 blocks moving each frame). A pixel no lamp lights (turned away from them, out of reach) is no receiver of the filter, as the sun's. The lamp's size is the [penumbra filter](render-shadows.md#traced-sun)'s: radius 2 units (a flame or a bulb in its glass), so the blocked lamps' widest penumbra at the receiver is 2 x the blocker's distance over its distance to the lamp. Rays go through the frame's TLAS and pass cut-out texels (`TracedSunShaders.CasterCutout`). Lamp data is this frame's, so carried lanterns are traced where they are drawn.
- **A lamp's own fixture.** Every ray stops 15 units short of the lamp (KenshiNative's near-skip) and at its radius. Interior lamps also pass through their emitter id's geometry (`GeometryRecord.Emitter`), as the probe trace does. Exterior lamps have no id: their placement is often the whole building, whose walls should block them.
- **Specular stays.** Only diffuse is removed. The surface target has no gloss, so the GGX term cannot be recomputed there; a lamp's highlight survives in its shadow. A dielectric's lamp specular is small; carrying gloss in the target's spare bits (10-15) would touch the world programs.
- **Casters.** Rays see everything the TLAS holds (`TracedSunShaders.LampMask`): terrain, objects (their cut-out parts by their own maps), foliage of radius 150 or more within 4000 units of the eye, and, built while the pass runs (lamps in the grid), the small foliage (radius 6 and up within 3000) and the grass (within 1200, or the [traced sun](render-shadows.md#traced-sun)'s reach) as for the traced sun, and the characters. Grass, small foliage and characters are absent from the probes' rays (`GiMask`).
- **Characters** (`TracedCharacters`, `CharacterRenderer.Rays`, mask `0x40`). Built for the lamps and the [traced sun](render-shadows.md#traced-sun). The nearest 128 within 4000 of the eye whose shadow can be in view, at most 3M triangles, at the level the colour pass draws (a surface's ray leaves the triangles it drew), with every shown part (clothes, armour, carried and drawn weapons, animals' meshes). A kernel skins each mesh group with the update's palettes, as the vertex program does (face morphs, bones, the look's hidden limbs and parts, headless, the boots' side), a triangle's three corners apiece; a triangle with a hidden corner collapses to a point. Per character one bottom level (fast build, refittable), refitted each frame its pose is new and built again every 60 to 75 refits or when its parts or levels change; its instance sits at the character's position. A character whose pose is held (frozen, or thinned by its size on screen: `CharState.Posed` unchanged) and whose pieces lie as they were skinned (within 0.05 units, same hidden parts and morphs) is neither skinned nor refitted. **Observed** (RTX 4090, 2560 x 1440, a crowd of 116 characters, 835k triangles): 50 held, 1.07 -> 0.80 ms of GPU; of that the kernel is 0.19 and the refits 0.61, which follow the triangles refitted (one command for all of them gained nothing). Hair is cut by its map's channel and threshold, items clipped on their normal map's alpha at 0.6, as the shadow draws. The top level is refitted while characters are in it, built again every 30 frames or when the instances change. The kernel runs one invocation a triangle over all of them (a job found by its first triangle). **Observed** (RTX 4090, 1920 x 1080 with DLSS, the new-game start in the Hub with `--crowd 60`, 110 characters, 326k triangles at drawn levels): 0.5 ms GPU a frame skinning and refitting, 0.6 with the top level, 62 MB; a lone close-up character (38k triangles) 0.4 ms. The GPU was shared with another job: runs varied 2-4x. A carried lantern's own carrier is not passed through (only the 15-unit near-skip): **Unknown** how its body's shadow looks.
- **Areas.** With the tiles the rays see the movers alone, and each lamp's pass works on the 64-pixel cells of the screen a mover's shadow can fall on (`LampShadowPass.PlanAreas`, `Classify`), from the mover's sphere. A character's sphere is its body's grown round every shown part that hangs from a bone, a tenth more (`CharacterRenderer.RayCharacterOf`): with the body's radius alone the shadow of a carried pole or a sheathed sword fell outside its cells and was cut along them, in blocks that came and went as characters walked. **Observed** (the Hub's first building at night, 2560 x 1440, 12 paused moments against spheres four times as large): the body's radius left 1774 and 665 pixels wrong in two of them; the grown sphere at most 232, the level of two captures of one sphere.
- **Characters as receivers.** While the pass will run (`GiProbes.LampReceivers`, `uGiParams.z` bit 16) a character's fragment writes its normal and albedo to the surface target with bit 19 (`kenshiLight`, `atmoReceiver` 3), which this pass alone reads (`LAMP_RECEIVER`): a hood shades the face, an arm the flank. Its rays leave as the [traced sun](render-shadows.md#traced-sun)'s do on a body (`lampBody`): close to the skin and past the creases between facets (`BodyContact`), and from under a facet turned from the lamp the ray crosses the body and is asked for blockers from where it leaves (`BodyChord`). Hair is no receiver (its cards shadowed each other black); it casts on the body as on everything else (a fringe shades the forehead). **Observed** (the editor's bar, a bare Greenlander and a clothed Scorchlander, 2560 x 1440): chin, hair mass and arms shade the body with no lines or facets; Skin's whole lamp term is taken back, its band past the terminator too: the fragment writes its skin share and its row of the scattering table (bits 28-31 and 20-27) and the pass computes the colour pass's own term (`LampShadowShaders.SkinTerm`, the shading normal standing for the smoothed ones). With N.L alone the band stayed and skin in a lamp's shadow glowed red (**Observed**, the editor's bar, a hooded face: red over green of a shadowed cheek 1.60 before, 1.44 after; 1.53 to 1.44 on a bare cheek). With the tiles every lamp a mover stands in is traced, one at a time (`LampShadowPass.RunPerLamp`); without them a pixel traces its two strongest and the others keep both. Skin's own highlights of a blocked lamp (`CharacterSurface.skinLight`) are left: taking them back changed nothing measurable on five skin zones of that scene. Cost **Unknown**.
- **Not covered.** Foliage, grass, hair, impostors and water write 0 to the surface target, so they keep unshadowed lamp light (interior surfaces too with `interiorgi` Faithful). Construction sites, impostor-only objects, objects past 6000 and foliage past 4000 (small 3000) of the eye, debris (bolts, dropped items), birds and particles cast nothing. The TLAS's meshes and 16-unit terrain grid are coarser than what is drawn: **Unknown** whether grazing rays show acne or leaks.

## Measured

- **Parity** (`image-diff`, 1280 × 720, `--view hub` and `--view swamp`, against `b84b372`):
  - with the lamps off (then `--lights off`): mean 0.0000 for both views;
  - with `--faithful gi` and the lamps off (then `--gi --faithful gi --lights off`): mean 0.0000 for both views;
  - with the lamps at noon: the Hub mean 0.0000, the swamp mean 0.03.
- **Cost** (RTX 4070, Mongrel at 22:00, 1920 × 1080 with DLSS 0.67, `--ab lights`): +0.07 ms GPU per frame.
- **Picture** (Mongrel at 22:00): small blue-white pools under the lamp posts, mean difference 0.38. The lights' radii are 60–300 units,
  so they are local.

## Lamp tiles

The still world's lamp shadows (`Gi/LampTiles`, console `lamps tiles on|off`): every lamp of a pixel is shadowed, with no limit on their number. Characters are not in the tiles.

- **Tiles.** Each lamp near the eye holds a distance tile traced from the lamp, in one atlas (8192², R16), and carried lamps a tile traced whole each frame (16 at most, first come).
- **Mapping.** A cone on its plane, a point lamp or a cone over 150° by octahedral directions. Side from reach and cone for about 1.25 units per texel at the end of the reach, halved past 1500 and 3500 units from the eye.
- **Invalidation.** `GiScene` keeps a hash per 128-unit cell of the object and foliage instances, so anything placed, removed or moved (a zone streamed in, a building built or ruined, a door) retraces the lamps whose reach holds that cell, 3 M rays a frame at most. An object that has moved in the last 60 frames is out of the tiles (instance mask `Moving`, what the probes and the sun still see) and in the per-pixel rays, so what keeps moving retraces nothing and a door's shadow follows it. A tile that must be retraced waits 2 frames between traces within 600 units of the eye, 16 beyond.
- **Ground.** Rays leave the ground where they come out of it (front faces culled): the traced grid is coarser than the drawn ground, and the first hit along a graze shaded the ground behind it (**Observed** in save spotlight-cross, as dark arcs round each lamp). Against 256 rays to the lamp's disc (console `lamps refview`), **Observed**: a lamp's shadow of objects matches (0.09 of 255 mean difference for a lamp post); a shadow of the ground's relief from a searchlight is narrower than the truth (6 of 255), and taking where the ray went into the ground for the penumbra's width made it worse (9.6).
- **Penumbra.** The source radius comes from the reach (0.005 of it, 1 to 2.5 units; the LIGHT record has no size field, **Verified** in `fcs.def` and [lights](formats/lights.md)). A spiral finds the blockers, a second one filters; a lookup r texels off counts only what stands nearer the lamp by what the surface can gain over r texels (its plane's depth at each lookup swung with the normal at a graze, and drew the ripples and the terrain's facets into the penumbra, **Observed**). Coarse levels of nearest blockers were tried and dropped: they read a slope's own near edge as a blocker, and drew squares (**Observed**, spotlight-cross).
- **Characters and moving objects.** One lamp at a time (`LampShadowPass.RunPerLamp`): over the part of the screen the movers' shadows from that lamp can reach, per-pixel rays, the penumbra filter on that one lamp's flags, and that lamp's light after the still world added to one sum taken off the scene. The rays' kernel asks only whether the lamp reaches the pixel (reach, cone, facing); the lamp's light after the still world (its tile's penumbra search and filter, the costly part) is computed by the last kernel, only where the filtered flag is above 0. A pixel the still world already shadows now carries its mover's flag into the filter, where it used to carry 0. The three kernels run over a list of 64-pixel cells, not the area's rectangle (`LampShadowShaders.Classify`, an indirect dispatch a kernel): a cell is listed when a mover's shadow box covers it and what this frame's depth pyramid shows in it (built after the opaque scene when the lamps' shadows run, `PostProcess.BuildHiz`) can lie within the lamp's reach and inside the shadow cone of a mover in that reach (a sphere round the cell's frustum between its nearest and farthest depth; over 64 such movers, or without a pyramid, the boxes alone decide), and so are its eight neighbours (the filter reads up to 30 pixels round a pixel). **Observed** (2560x1440, town at night, 19 lamps' areas, 54 movers): the stage 2.9 -> 2.45 ms with the light computed last, -> 1.78 ms with the lists (0.32 ms with no cell listed, 2.56 with every cell); spotlight-cross 2.5 -> 1.86 ms. A mover standing at its lamp (a carried lantern) keeps every cell in the lamp's reach.
- **Traced light.** The probes, the GI cache and the traced reflections read a lamp's tile at their hits, one filtered lookup: every lamp respects the still world in the bounce. A lamp with no tile keeps `giLampVisibility` (a ray for an interior lamp, none for the others).
